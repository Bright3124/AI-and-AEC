About Me

I am a structural engineer focused on bridging the gap between traditional architectural and engineering practices and the next generation of AI tools. My work revolves around project design, coordination meetings, and finding ways to work smarter, not harder.

From sizing wood posts and designing reinforced concrete shear walls to managing complex code requirements (ASCE, ACI, AISC), I leverage AI and automation tools like Microsoft Power Automate to streamline workflows, allowing for more focus on critical design analysis.

AEC Use Cases & Projects

Structural Engineering Assistant

A custom AI agent configured specifically for structural designers. It acts as a rapid knowledge base for code compliance, specifically trained to reference ASCE 7 and ACI 318.

Key Feature: The agent is programmed with a priority directive to first check internal resources before utilizing broader data. Test validation included specific prompts such as querying for minimum slab reinforcement limits.

Week 4: The Hallucination Trap & Behavioral Calibration

Summary: An analysis of "Why Language Models Hallucinate" (Kalai et al., 2025). The research proves hallucinations are statistical inevitabilities, worsened by benchmarks that incentivize AI to guess rather than admit ignorance. For high-stakes AEC applications where precision is paramount, models require strict "behavioral calibration" and heavy penalties for confident errors, as standard Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) cannot fix the core incentive to guess on unique project data.

Week 5: The Agentic Shift, BIM Operating Systems, and the Gigawatt Ceiling

Summary: The AI industry is moving past standalone software into an "Agent-as-a-Service" model, where autonomous multi-agent systems execute complex workflows. In the AEC sector, this manifests as "Agentic BIM," transitioning file-based applications into a cloud-based "operating system" where AI continuously generates and tests structural and MEP elements for code compliance. As agents handle the heavy lifting, the human role will pivot from software operator to an "orchestrator" defining boundaries and design intent. Concurrently, the massive computational power required for these systems is hitting a "Gigawatt Ceiling," sparking a datacenter construction boom that will heavily rely on specialized structural engineering to support advanced cooling and energy loads. Ultimately, this extreme efficiency will force AEC firms to shift their business models from billable hours to fixed-fee, value-based pricing.
